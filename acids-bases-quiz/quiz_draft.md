# Acids & Bases Quiz: Content Draft (30 questions)

Qualitative only (no pKa). Rules: A = atom (same row: electronegativity; same column: size), R = resonance, I = induction, O = orbital. Order of priority is A > R > I > O. "Structure" lines give SMILES-style notes for the drawing step.
Difficulty: D = direct (one factor), T = two factors, H = hardest. Answer key letters are spread 7/7/8/8 across A-D.

---

## Part 1: Atom (Q1-6)

**1 (D)** Ethanolamine has three kinds of H, labeled below. Which H is most acidic?
Structure: HO-CH2-CH2-NH2 with Ha = C-H next to O, Hb = N-H, Hc = O-H.
A) Ha  B) Hb  C) Hc  D) All three are about equally acidic
**Answer: C.** Same row, so electronegativity decides. O is the most electronegative, so the O⁻ conjugate base is most stable.

**2 (D)** Which is the stronger acid, H₂O or H₂S, and why?
A) H₂O, because O is more electronegative than S
B) H₂S, because S is larger and spreads the negative charge over a greater volume
C) H₂O, because the small size of O concentrates the charge and stabilizes it
D) They are equal because O and S are in the same column
**Answer: B.** Same column, so size beats electronegativity.

**3 (D)** Rank from weakest to strongest acid: HF, HCl, HBr, HI.
A) HI < HBr < HCl < HF
B) HF < HCl < HI < HBr
C) HCl < HF < HBr < HI
D) HF < HCl < HBr < HI
**Answer: D.** Same column. The larger anion is more stable.

**4 (D)** Which anion is the weakest base?
Structure: A) F⁻  B) HO⁻  C) ⁻NH₂  D) ⁻CH₃
**Answer: A.** Same row. F is the most electronegative, so F⁻ is the most stable and the weakest base.

**5 (T)** In the molecule below, which labeled H is most acidic?
Structure: HO-CH2-CH2-CH2-SH with Ha = O-H, Hb = S-H, Hc = C-H next to S, Hd = C-H next to O.
A) Ha, because O is more electronegative than S
B) Hb, because S is larger than O
C) Hc, because it is closest to the large S atom
D) Ha and Hb are equally acidic
**Answer: B.** Same column, so size dominates and HS⁻ is more stable than HO⁻.

**6 (D)** Ethanol is a stronger acid than ethane. Which statement explains this?
A) The O–H bond is longer than the C–H bond
B) Ethoxide has resonance structures and the ethyl anion does not
C) O is more electronegative than C, so CH₃CH₂O⁻ is more stable than CH₃CH₂⁻
D) CH₃CH₂O⁻ has an sp-hybridized atom
**Answer: C.**

## Part 2: Resonance (Q7-12)

**7 (D)** Which is the stronger acid, ethanol or acetic acid, and why?
Structure: CH3CH2OH vs CH3C(=O)OH
A) Acetic acid, because its conjugate base has the charge delocalized over two oxygens
B) Ethanol, because its conjugate base is more localized and stable
C) Acetic acid, because the carbonyl carbon is more electronegative than the CH₂ carbon
D) They are equal because both acidic H atoms are on oxygen
**Answer: A.**

**8 (T)** Which labeled H is most acidic?
Structure: CH3-NH-CH2-CH2-C(=O)-NH-CH3 with Ha = amine N-H, Hb = amide N-H, Hc = CH2 next to C=O, Hd = CH2 next to amine N.
A) Ha  B) Hc  C) Hd  D) Hb
**Answer: D.** Amide N⁻ is delocalized onto O. Hc is a C-H, so atom (factor 1) rules it out.

**9 (D)** Phenol is a stronger acid than cyclohexanol. Why?
A) Cyclohexanol has a larger O–H bond
B) The two have the same acidity, since both have an O–H
C) The negative charge of phenoxide is delocalized into the ring by resonance
D) The OH on an sp² carbon makes the oxygen more electronegative
**Answer: C.**

**10 (T)** Which labeled H is most acidic?
Structure: CH3-C(=O)-CH2-C(=O)-CH2-CH3 with Ha = H on the left CH3, Hb = H on the central CH2, Hc = H on the ethyl CH2.
A) Ha  B) Hb  C) Hc  D) All are equally acidic
**Answer: B.** All three are carbon acids (factor 1 is tied). Only Hb gives a conjugate base delocalized onto two carbonyl oxygens.

**11 (T)** The conjugate base of acetic acid (acetate) is drawn with its first resonance structure. Which set of curved arrows correctly converts it to the second resonance structure?
Structure: CH3-C(=O)-O⁻ → CH3-C(-O⁻)=O
A) Arrow 1: tail on a lone pair of O⁻, head between C and O (forming a C=O). Arrow 2: tail on the C=O π bond, head on the other O. **(correct)**
B) Same two arrows as A, but both drawn pointing backward (heads on the atoms, tails on the bonds)
C) A single arrow with its tail on the O⁻ and its head on the CH₃ carbon
D) Arrow 1 as in A. Arrow 2: tail on the C=O π bond, head on the carbonyl carbon
**Answer: A.** The drawing must show the arrows in each option.

**12 (H)** Rank these from least acidic to most acidic: ethanol, phenol, acetic acid.
A) acetic acid < phenol < ethanol
B) phenol < ethanol < acetic acid
C) ethanol < acetic acid < phenol
D) ethanol < phenol < acetic acid
**Answer: D.** Ethanol has no resonance. Phenoxide spreads the charge over one O and carbons. Acetate spreads it over two oxygens.

## Part 3: Induction (Q13-16)

**13 (D)** Rank from least acidic to most acidic: I = CH₃CO₂H, II = ClCH₂CO₂H, III = Cl₃CCO₂H.
A) III < II < I  B) II < I < III  C) I < II < III  D) I < III < II
**Answer: C.**

**14 (D)** Which labeled H is most acidic?
Structure: HO-C(CF3)2-CH2-CH2-CH2-OH with Ha = O-H on the carbon bearing the two CF₃ groups, Hb = O-H at the far end.
A) Ha, because the CF₃ groups stabilize the nearby conjugate base by induction
B) Hb, because primary alcohols are less hindered
C) Hb, because it is farther from the electron-withdrawing groups
D) They are equally acidic
**Answer: A.**

**15 (T)** Which is more acidic: 2-chlorobutanoic acid or 4-chlorobutanoic acid?
Structure: CH3CH2CH(Cl)CO2H vs ClCH2CH2CH2CO2H
A) 4-chlorobutanoic acid, because Cl is farther from the acidic H
B) 2-chlorobutanoic acid, because the inductive effect weakens with distance
C) They are equal because each has one Cl
D) 4-chlorobutanoic acid, because Cl is less sterically hindered
**Answer: B.**

**16 (D)** Which acid is the strongest?
Structure: A) CH3CO2H  B) BrCH2CO2H  C) ICH2CO2H  D) FCH2CO2H
**Answer: D.** The halogen is on carbon, so the most electronegative one withdraws electron density best.

## Part 4: Orbitals (Q17-20)

**17 (D)** Rank from least acidic to most acidic: ethane, ethene, ethyne.
A) ethyne < ethene < ethane
B) ethene < ethane < ethyne
C) ethane < ethene < ethyne
D) ethane < ethyne < ethene
**Answer: C.**

**18 (D)** Which labeled H is most acidic?
Structure: HC≡C-CH2-CH2-CH=CH2 with Ha = alkyne H, Hb = vinyl H on the internal CH=, Hc = vinyl H on the terminal =CH₂, Hd = H on a CH₂.
A) Ha  B) Hb  C) Hc  D) Hd
**Answer: A.** Only factor 4 differentiates here.

**19 (T)** Which anion is the strongest base?
Structure: A) CH3(CH2)3C≡C⁻  B) CH3(CH2)3CH=CH⁻  C) all three are equally strong  D) CH3(CH2)4CH2⁻
**Answer: D.** The charge sits in an sp³ orbital, the highest-energy case.

**20 (D)** In a molecule that has both, why is a terminal alkyne H more acidic than a vinyl H?
A) The sp carbon has more p character, so it is more electronegative
B) The electron pair in an sp orbital is held closer to the nucleus (more s character)
C) The alkynide ion has resonance structures
D) The triple bond withdraws electron density from the H by induction
**Answer: B.**

## Part 5: Competing factors (Q21-24)

**21 (T)** Which is more acidic: the O–H of ethanol or an allylic C–H of propene (the CH₃ hydrogens)?
Structure: CH3CH2OH vs CH2=CH-CH3
A) Propene, because the allyl anion is resonance stabilized
B) They are about equal
C) Ethanol, because the atom (O vs C) outranks resonance
D) Propene, because the carbon is sp²-hybridized
**Answer: C.**

**22 (H)** Which labeled H is most acidic?
Structure: HO2C-CH2-CH2-CH2-CH(OH)-CF3 with Ha = carboxylic acid O–H, Hb = alcohol O–H on the carbon bearing CF₃.
A) Hb, because the CF₃ stabilizes the conjugate base by induction
B) Ha, because resonance outranks induction
C) They are equal
D) Hb, because alcohols are more acidic than carboxylic acids
**Answer: B.**

**23 (H)** Which labeled H is most acidic?
Structure: HC≡C-CH2-CH2-OH with Ha = alkyne H, Hb = O–H.
A) Hb, because the atom (O vs C) outranks orbital
B) Ha, because the sp orbital outranks the atom
C) Ha, because the alkynide ion is resonance stabilized
D) They are equal
**Answer: A.**

**24 (H)** Rank from least acidic to most acidic: I = ethanol, II = trifluoroethanol (CF₃CH₂OH), III = acetic acid.
A) I < III < II  B) III < II < I  C) II < I < III  D) I < II < III
**Answer: D.** I vs II: induction. III: resonance beats induction.

## Part 6: Position of equilibrium (Q25-30)

**25 (D)** Which side is favored?
Structure: CH3CH2O⁻ + CH3C(=O)OH ⇌ CH3CH2OH + CH3C(=O)O⁻
A) The reactants, because ethoxide is more stable
B) Neither side is favored
C) The products, because acetate is resonance stabilized and is the more stable anion
D) The products, because ethanol is the stronger acid
**Answer: C.**

**26 (T)** Which side is favored?
Structure: HO⁻ + CH3CH2SH ⇌ H2O + CH3CH2S⁻
A) The products, because the larger S atom better stabilizes the negative charge
B) The reactants, because O is more electronegative than S
C) The reactants, because hydroxide is the smaller anion
D) It cannot be predicted
**Answer: A.**

**27 (T)** Which side is favored?
Structure: CH3CH2O⁻ + HC≡CH ⇌ CH3CH2OH + HC≡C⁻
A) The products, because the sp carbanion is the most stable
B) The reactants, because the alkoxide (charge on O) is more stable than the alkynide (charge on C)
C) The products, because the alkynide has resonance structures
D) Neither, because both have a charge on a single atom
**Answer: B.**

**28 (T)** Which side is favored?
Structure: C6H5O⁻ + CH3CH2OH ⇌ C6H5OH + CH3CH2O⁻
A) The products, because ethanol is the more acidic reactant
B) The products, because ethoxide is more stable
C) Neither, because both anions have the charge on O
D) The reactants, because the phenoxide is resonance stabilized
**Answer: D.**

**29 (H)** Which side is favored?
Structure: CF3CO2⁻ + CH3CO2H ⇌ CF3CO2H + CH3CO2⁻
A) The reactants, because CF₃CO₂⁻ is stabilized by induction
B) The products, because acetate is resonance stabilized
C) The products, because CH₃ donates electron density to the carboxylate
D) Neither, because both anions are resonance stabilized
**Answer: A.**

**30 (T)** Which set of curved arrows correctly shows the proton transfer below?
Structure: HO⁻ + phenol (C6H5-O-H) → H2O + phenoxide
A) One arrow only, from the O–H bond to the O of phenol
B) Arrow 1: tail on the H, head on the hydroxide O. Arrow 2: tail on the phenol O.
C) Arrow 1: tail on a lone pair of HO⁻, head on the H. Arrow 2: tail on the O–H bond, head on the O. **(correct)**
D) Arrow 1: tail on a lone pair of HO⁻, head on the phenol O. Arrow 2: tail on the O–H bond, head on the H
**Answer: C.** The drawing must show the arrows in each option.
