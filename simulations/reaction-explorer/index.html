Reading the picture

  • Soft glow = electron cloud. Where two clouds merge, electrons are shared: that is a covalent bond.
  • Dashed line = a bond that is only partly formed or partly broken.
  • Pull arrows point toward each other (attraction). Push arrows point apart (repulsion). Longer arrow = stronger force.
  • δ+ / δ− = partial charges. Electrons spend more time near the more electronegative atom.

Glossary

Activation energy (Ea)
The minimum energy a collision needs to reach the top of the hill. It is a threshold, not the overall energy change.
Transition state (‡)
The highest-energy arrangement on the way from reactants to products. Old bond half broken, new bond half made. It exists for only a few femtoseconds.
ΔH (enthalpy change)
Energy of products minus energy of reactants. Negative = exothermic (energy released), positive = endothermic (energy absorbed).
Effective collision
A collision with enough energy and the right orientation, so it makes products.
Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution
How kinetic energy is shared among the molecules at one temperature. A few are slow, a few are very fast, most are in between.
Catalyst
Offers a different route with a lower activation energy. It does not change ΔH and is not used up.
Femtosecond (fs)
10⁻¹⁵ s. A femtosecond is to one second what one second is to about 32 million years.

How real is it? (for teachers)

  • The energies come from a real potential energy surface (the LEPS model used in research on these reactions), built from each bond's energy and length and tuned to the measured activation energy.
  • The atoms follow the minimum-energy path across that surface and obey Newton's laws along it, so kinetic + potential energy is conserved and times are in real femtoseconds.
  • Groups such as CH₃ are treated as one particle. Solvent is not shown for the SN2 reaction and its numbers are approximate.
  • Real molecules also tunnel, rotate and vibrate before colliding. Those are left out to keep the picture clear.
  • In view ②, kinetic energies follow the true 3-D Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution and a collision reacts when the energy along the line of centers is at least Ea, which gives the Arrhenius factor e−Ea/RT.
